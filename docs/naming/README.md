# Naming study: candidates to replace `byconvo`

Brief: a name in the register of Cursor, Warp and Zed — one or two syllables, a real word,
hard edges. **100 candidates**, each with at least one registrable domain.

326 words × 8 patterns = **2,608 domains** probed 2026-08-25. 474 came back unregistered,
spread over 205 of the 326 words. **Not one bare `.com` was free** — the `run-`, `get-`,
`use-` and `try-` prefixes carry the entire list.

`?` marks a domain the resolvers could not settle either way. See *Method* below.

## Method, and what `FREE` is worth

`dns-availability-check.py` sends a UDP `NS` query per domain, rotating across twelve public
recursors (Google, Cloudflare, Quad9, OpenDNS, AdGuard and others). Every `NXDOMAIN` is
corroborated by a follow-up `SOA` and `A` query before the name is called free. Validated
against known-registered and known-free control names first.

**`FREE` is strong evidence, not proof.** It means no nameservers exist for the name. A domain
can be registered and left undelegated, or sit in redemption after expiry, and still answer
`NXDOMAIN`. Confirm at a registrar before committing.

RDAP — the authoritative registration check — was unavailable: the network egress policy blocks
`rdap.verisign.com` and its mirrors, along with DNS-over-HTTPS and TCP port 53. UDP DNS was the
only channel.

65 of 2,608 domains returned `SERVFAIL` from all twelve resolvers, serialized and with DNSSEC
checking disabled. Since genuinely free names always answered clean `NXDOMAIN`, these are more
likely registered than not — but they are marked `?` rather than guessed at.

Trademark and existing-product collisions were not searched exhaustively. Clear the finalist
properly before filing.

## Reproducing

```sh
while read w; do
  for p in "$w.com" "run$w.com" "get$w.com" "use$w.com" \
           "try$w.com" "$w.dev" "$w.app" "$w.sh"; do echo "$p"; done
done < candidate-words.txt | python3 dns-availability-check.py > results.json
```
